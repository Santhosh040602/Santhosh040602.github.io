Abstract

Growing IoT deployments have created an urgent need for compression techniques that can operate within tight bandwidth, low energy, and computational constraints of LoRa-based sensor networks. Sending high-dimensional data, such as images, over LoRa remains difficult given its low data rates, susceptibility to channel noise, and the limited processing capabilities of edge hardware. To address this, a disjoint autoencoder framework is presented in which a differentiable LoRa channel module is incorporated into the training process, allowing the encoder and decoder to learn latent representations that withstand real-world LoRa transmission impairments. After training, the encoder and decoder are deployed at the transmitter and the receiver, supporting asymmetric operation that can be tailored to specific bandwidth, complexity, and energy constraints. The framework is thoroughly assessed on the well-known CIFAR-10 dataset spanning a broad set of architectural configurations, and then physically tested on Raspberry Pi Zero 2W devices in outdoor LoRa environments. The results show that the proposed framework produces substantially more resilient reconstruction quality at extended transmission ranges than what conventional compression approaches can achieve.
